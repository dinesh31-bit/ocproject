A Field Guide To The Wild

Faces of the Wild

From apex predators to the forests that shelter them — eighteen species and ecosystems, organized the way life itself is organized: by kind and by home.

Category

Mammals

Warm-blooded, fiercely adaptable — the mammals share our world from mountain ridge to savanna floor.

Bengal Tiger

Panthera tigris tigris — an apex predator of South Asia's mangroves and grasslands, relying on stealth and powerful hind legs to close the final ambush.

Mammal · Endangered

African Elephant

Loxodonta africana — Earth's largest land mammal, using a trunk of over 40,000 muscles to lift logs, draw water, and greet family members.

Mammal · Endangered

Gray Wolf

Canis lupus — a highly social pack hunter whose long-distance howls coordinate movement and defend territory across forest and tundra.

Mammal · Least Concern

Snow Leopard

Panthera uncia — the "ghost of the mountains," whose smoky rosettes and long tail grant balance on the steepest Himalayan slopes.

Mammal · Vulnerable

Category

Birds

Color, song and impossible flight — birds have colonized every sky on the planet.

Blue-and-Yellow Macaw

Ara ararauna — a vividly plumed parrot of South American rainforests that mates for life and can live more than 30 years in the wild.

Bird · Least Concern

Harpy Eagle

Harpia harpyja — one of the world's most powerful raptors, its talons rivaling a grizzly's claws when snatching sloths from the canopy.

Bird · Vulnerable

Snowy Owl

Bubo scandiacus — an Arctic hunter whose thick white plumage insulates against brutal winters, hunting by daylight unlike most owls.

Bird · Vulnerable

Category

Marine Life

Beneath the surface, an entire living world moves in currents older than the continents.

Loggerhead Sea Turtle

Caretta caretta — powerful jaws crush hard-shelled prey, and migrates thousands of miles between feeding grounds and natal beaches.

Marine · Vulnerable

Common Octopus

Octopus vulgaris — a master of camouflage and problem-solving, changing skin texture and color in a fraction of a second to vanish among reef.

Marine · Least Concern

Orca (Killer Whale)

Orcinus orca — a highly intelligent apex predator that travels in tight-knit matrilineal pods, using coordinated strategies to hunt.

Marine · Near Threatened

Category

Reptiles

Cold-blooded survivors that have outlasted ice ages, still ruling riverbanks and islands alike.

Panther Chameleon

Furcifer pardalis — a Madagascar native famed for dazzling color shifts driven as much by mood and temperature as by camouflage.

Reptile · Least Concern

Saltwater Crocodile

Crocodylus porosus — the largest living reptile, an ambush predator patrolling estuaries and coastlines from India to Australia.

Reptile · Least Concern

Galápagos Tortoise

Chelonoidis niger — a giant island tortoise that can live over a century, shaping its ecosystem through grazing and seed dispersal.

Reptile · Vulnerable

Category

Insects

Small in size, immense in impact — insects pollinate, recycle and sustain nearly every ecosystem on Earth.

Monarch Butterfly

Danaus plexippus — famed for a multigenerational migration spanning thousands of miles between North America and Mexican overwintering forests.

Insect · Endangered

European Honeybee

Apis mellifera — a keystone pollinator whose waggle dance communicates precise flower locations to the rest of the hive.

Insect · Least Concern

Green Darner Dragonfly

Anax junius — a swift aerial predator and one of the few dragonflies known to migrate seasonally across North America.

Insect · Least Concern

Category

Forests & Ecosystems

Not every face of the wild has fur or feathers — these living landscapes shelter everything above.

Giant Sequoia Grove

Sequoiadendron giganteum — among the most massive living organisms on Earth, protected by thick, fire-resistant bark that lets groves outlast the blazes that clear competing growth.

Forest · Endangered

Mangrove Forest

Rhizophora spp. — tangled root systems buffer coastlines from storms while nursing juvenile fish and shrimp among their submerged roots.

Forest · Vulnerable

Rainforest Canopy

Amazon Basin — a layered ecosystem hosting more biodiversity per hectare than almost anywhere on Earth, home to an estimated 10% of known species.

Forest · Critically Endangered
